LTV / CAC

A saver won online costs ₹495 and returns ₹3,813 over five years: 7.7× LTV/CAC, paid back in month 12

7.7× in 5 years, 12.2× in 8LTV/CAC = what a saver brings in, less the cost of serving them, divided by the ₹495 it cost to win them. Undiscounted.
Cross-sell is 88% of revenueLoans ₹3,686 (personal, home, property, gold, durables), insurance ₹0, cards and other products, on top of ₹531 of fund fees.
Doorstep: 5.6×₹446 to win all-in; 55% still saving at a year; pays back in month 18. The 1 in 10 test.
The plan: ₹100 a day; 38% still saving at a year (our extrapolation from SBI MF's ₹250 daily SIP); 80/20 equity and debt funds. Serving = ₹5 support, ₹4 tech for each of the 2.5 KYC'd users and ₹2 a weekly debit, a month while saving; the cost to win includes our 10% contingency. Not in this view: the company's fixed costs (team, office, brand), which is why the company turns profitable later than the saver. LTV/CAC is 7.7× in the plan; ₹300 a day takes it to 9.8× and ₹300 to win to 11.6×, while savings-app retention cuts it to 4.9×

Loans move it mostIf only 10% apply a year it falls to 2.3×; with offers to savers only (not every KYC'd user) 3.5×.
Cost to win is ours to drive downOur funnel today is ₹600 (5.8×); the plan needs ₹450; a competitor reports ₹300 (11.6×).
Retention still matters38% is our extrapolation; at 24%, like savings apps, LTV/CAC is 4.9×, because fewer people stay to borrow.
LTV/CAC = a saver's revenue less serving cost over 60 months, divided by the cost to win; undiscounted. Company profitable = the month EBITDA turns positive at 20,000 online savers a month (the plan). The 24% case also lowers month-3 retention to 52%.
