The key design choice

The savings instrument decides the business: gold pays for the daily habit, but only an FD can back a card or a loan.

Gold is the only home for daily amounts under the ₹100 fund minimum, and the only one that earns real money per rupee saved. It is also the one we can't lend against, and nobody regulates it.

Say: This is the most important design choice, and your brief flagged it. Gold takes ₹10, so it is the only home for small daily saves; it pays a distributor about 1.5% of each purchase in our base case (Jar reportedly earned about 2.4% as a distributor, unverified); and rewards on it are not prohibited, while fund distributors may not offer rebates or gifts. But RBI bars lending against gold, including digital gold, from April 2026, SEBI says it is outside its rules, and the saver pays a 2-3% spread plus 3% GST, so gold sold within a year loses money. A liquid fund earns us only 0.08% a year, about ₹4 on ₹5,000. A bank FD can back a secured card, but only at the same bank. Likely question: Why not funds only, and avoid gold's risk? Answer: Then daily saves must be at least ₹100, and savings revenue is close to zero. That is the idea as briefed, and it is weaker in every case.
