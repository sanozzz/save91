Say: Bueno lent small three-month loans to blue-collar and gig workers, mostly through platforms like Swiggy. At our peak we made 15,000+ loans a month with 60 people. A partner NBFC funded the loans at 18% a year and we covered all its losses. Borrowers paid about 50% a year including fees. Walk the bars: on one ₹17,500 first loan we kept ₹1,382 of interest and fees, paid −₹528 to the partner, lost −₹523 on late loans (6-7% of loans went 90+ days late and we recovered about half), and spent on collections, operations and the platform's data fee. The first loan ends at −₹139, before the ₹760 it cost to find the borrower. Repeat loans were better, but only 30-50% of repayers came back. Likely question: How did Bueno end? Answer: BetterPlace acquired it in May 2023. The lesson I took: platforms were the best channel, the employer channel was too small, and the first loan never paid for itself.
