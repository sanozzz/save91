# Cross-sell other than loans: what each saver could bring in

Researched 2 Oct 2026. Every number comes from a row in `xs_other.csv` (XO-01 to XO-39). Each row gives the source, the date and the group of people it describes. Rows marked **derived** are my own arithmetic on published numbers. Take-up rates are **my judgment**, because nobody publishes them for this population. Each one is anchored on the company figures in section 2.

## Bottom line

Taken together, non-loan products bring in about **Rs 63 a year per active saver in the middle case** (range Rs 8 to Rs 259). The gap to break even is **Rs 1,029** (Rs 1,168 needed minus Rs 139 today), so they close about **6%** of it. Even if every product hit its best case at the same time, they would close only about 25%. **No non-loan product closes the 9x gap.**

## 2. Cross-check against real companies

- **PhonePe** (FY26 filings): its insurance arm earned **Rs 191 crore**. Spread over about 23.7 crore monthly active users, that is **Rs 8 per active user per year**. Wealth earned **Rs 4.6**. Insurance revenue grew only 6% in the year (XO-10). Our middle case for accident and health cover (Rs 7) sits at the same level.
- **Paytm** (Q1 FY27 results, primary): Paytm says mutual funds, gold savings and add-on insurance "**contribute negligible revenue/profitability**". Its Rs 407 a year per monthly user from financial services comes almost entirely from loans and share trading (XO-11).
- **Groww**: India's biggest mutual-fund app sells only direct plans, so it earns **about zero commission** on Rs 1.1 lakh crore of its clients' fund money. It makes its money from share trading (XO-36).
- **Policybazaar** (FY26): revenue was **20% of premium** (Rs 6,089 crore on Rs 29,934 crore), and 89% of health policies renewed. That is the best case in the market, and it comes from middle-class buyers paying several thousand rupees, not daily earners (XO-09).
- **Prudent** (large fund distributor, FY26): earned about **0.9% a year** on mostly equity funds, before paying its sub-agents (XO-08). Liquid funds pay 10-20x less.
- **Choti SIP nationally**: only **322,000** have been started in 17 months. For every 28,000 started each month, about 20,000 close. SBI's Rs 250 SIP lost **36% of a year's sign-ups** within the year (XO-04, XO-05).

## 3. What this means for the model

- **The model's insurance line may be too high.** In the middle case it earns about **Rs 70 per saver in year 1**: 90% eligible x 21.5% buy in the year x Rs 3,000 premium x 12%. That is about 9x what PhonePe earns per active user (XO-38). The weakest input is the **Rs 3,000 premium**. What this group actually buys costs Rs 20 to Rs 1,500 (PMSBY Rs 20, rider accident cover Rs 50, hospital cash Rs 799, Bima Vistaar Rs 1,500). A premium of Rs 800-1,500 with 6-10% buying a year would be closer to the evidence.
- **Mutual funds:** the Rs 500 Choti incentive is real but conditional. It needs exactly Rs 250 a month into an equity or hybrid fund, kept for 24 months, and it is lost on a single bounce. It **cannot** apply to daily saving in a liquid fund. Expect Rs 75-200 per Choti SIP started, paid in year 2 or later (XO-01, XO-04).
- **Shared government schemes** (PMSBY, PMJJBY, APY, e-Shram) are worth offering as free benefits that build trust. They pay the app almost nothing, and the rules route the money to banks and CSCs.

## 4. Risks

**Regulatory**
- **Insurance commission caps are coming.** IRDAI's consultation paper (23 Sep 2026; comments close 25 Oct 2026) proposes these caps for open-shelf distributors: 15% on the first sale of health, accident or shopkeeper cover; **5% on health renewals**; **nil to 2.5% on third-party motor**; **5% on any insurance sold with a loan**; 7.5-25% on term life. It also proposes clawbacks and making each seller accountable. Today's payouts (up to 61% in year 1 at some corporate agents) would not survive this (XO-18 to XO-20; regulation_rails REG-053 to REG-057).
- **Mutual-fund rules:** a distributor may not pass on commission as rewards (LEV-043, REG-082). Promotion with influencers must stay education-only (OWN-03). Choti SIP asks for a 60-instalment commitment, has exit loads, and allows monthly debits only. For a customer who needs cash at short notice, that is a suitability risk.
- **NPS money is locked until age 60.** Selling it as "savings" to a daily earner would be mis-selling.
- **Licences cost money and time.** A corporate agent dealing only in insurance needs Rs 50 lakh of net worth (REG-059). The proposed IDE licence needs Rs 10 lakh of capital (REG-057). An NPS PoP needs PFRDA registration. Each licence adds compliance staff for single-digit rupees per saver.

**Reputational**
- **Duplicate cover.** Many riders already get free accident cover from Zomato, Blinkit or Swiggy (tri_costs TC-26/27). Platform workers registered on e-Shram get PM-JAY health cover of Rs 5 lakh (REG-060). Selling paid cover that overlaps would look like taking advantage of people.
- **Sales hurting savings.** First-time investors stop when markets fall: Choti SIP closures run at 71% of new starts (XO-04). A loss in the first equity SIP a saver ever holds could damage trust in the savings product itself.
- **Low renewal and claim friction.** Older Indian studies put voluntary micro-insurance renewal anywhere from 4% to 61% (XO-24). Cheap hospital-cash and device policies are known for disputed claims (not measured here). One rejected claim spreads fast in a rider WhatsApp group.
