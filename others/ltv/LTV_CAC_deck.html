THE DEFINITION

One saver's value is three streams (the fund, loans, other products) for as long as they keep saving, less serving, against the cost to win them

Saver won, not sign-upA saver is someone who starts saving. Online, 1 in 5 sign-ups does; the cost to win is everything spent to get one saver.
Undiscounted, 5 years (8 alongside)LTV = what a saver brings in less what it costs to serve them while they save. No discounting: the cumulative rupees.
The cautious saverThe answer is for a saver who joins early, before take-up and approvals have grown with the business's track record (they grow to year 5), at the post-seed ₹450 to win (₹495 with our 10% contingency). Later savers are worth more.
Words used here. KYC'd: finished the identity check. Trail: the yearly share of a fund's fee paid to its distributor. SIP: a regular instalment into a mutual fund. NBFC: a non-bank lender. DSA: an agent paid by a lender for referred loans. EMI: a loan instalment. LAP: a loan against property. Doorstep: a field agent signs the saver in person. The seed: the seed round, an 18-month plan plus 6 months to raise the next round. Futures: simulated draws of all inputs together.
Not in this view: the team, office and brand, the two-month wait for fees, tax. Fees are quoted before GST and TDS. The app never holds the money; loans are made by banks and NBFCs.

THE CREDIT BET

7.7× holds only if one bet comes true

The bet: our savers earn more and want credit, and their saving history makes lenders say yes more often and lend more. If it is true, loan size, how many borrow and what lenders pay rise together; if it is false, they fall together.
Why it could be trueDaily savers who ask to see loan offers are the most credit-ready users an app has, and a saving record is data a lender can use.
Why it may not beOur interviews put savers at ₹20–40k a month. Higher earners borrow bigger, but investing apps pay ₹800–1,400 to win them.
How we will knowFive checks, most before any money is lent (two pages on): pre-screen, terms, who signs up, opt-in, cost to win.
Market: first loans ₹5,000–25,000 and 1–5% of app users borrowing a year (Paytm, Kissht and PhonePe filings, 2025–26); Kissht approves 11% of new applicants (prospectus, 2026); the largest investing app pays ₹800–1,400 per new investor (shareholder letters, 2025–26); DSA rate cards 1–2.5%; still saving after a year 24–45%. Income needed: the EMI at 24% a year over 18 months, at 40–50% of income.

A lender's pre-screen. A lending partner soft-checks the credit of our first 1,000 KYC'd sign-ups: the approval rate and the loan each could get. Weeks, and no money lent.
2
Terms in writing. The payout, clawbacks and approval rules for savers with 3–6 months of daily saving, in a signed letter.
3
Who signs up. The income of the first sign-ups. It sets the loan size more than anything we assume.
4
Opt-in and apply. How many KYC'd users ask to see lender offers, and how many apply in their first 3 months.
5
The real cost to win. What the pilot pays per saver who makes a first debit, month by month.
The pass mark: lender fees on track for ₹1,358 per saver won for 3×, ₹368 to break even (the one-number page). If checks 1 and 2 come back strong, the bet has evidence behind it; if they come back at market levels, we know before the money goes into scale.
One target misses: only that input changes. Two or three miss: the inputs named change together. Today's market rates: as on the one-number page. A soft credit check reads a bureau file without touching the person's score.

A11 · HOW THE MODEL WORKS

The month-by-month rules behind one saver's value

StepHow it is worked out, every month
Who keeps savingA steady monthly drop-off within months 1–3, another within months 4–12 (set by the share at month 3 and at month 12), then 1.5% a month.
The fundBalance = last month's balance less 1% withdrawn, plus deposits, plus the blended return on both. Our trail = average balance × 0.67% ÷ 12.
LoansEach reachable user is in one of three states: never borrowed, repaid and able to borrow again, or repaying. Each month a share applies (35% a year spread over 12 months, × the take-up factor); approved first loans move to repaying; 18 months later they can borrow again.
Top-upsBorrowers with 12+ EMIs paid and EMIs still due: 15% a year take a top-up of 40% of the original loan, at most one per loan.
PayoutsMoney lent × the payout (2% rising in a straight line to 3% by month 60) × (1 − 2.5% taken back). Counted for 2.5 KYC'd users per online saver.
ProductsFor each product, the share not yet holding it buys at a monthly rate (the yearly take-up spread over 12 months × the take-up factor). Policies renew yearly at their renewal rate; habits keep running; insurance counts a fixed referral fee, to be agreed (₹0 here); the Choti SIP incentive and digital gold are off.
FactorsTake-up factor: 50% in months 1–6, then 100%, × 1.05 per business year to year 5 (cap 2×). First-loan approval: 30% × 60% in months 1–6 × 1.10 per year (cap 80%).
Serving and paybackServing = share still saving × (₹5 support + ₹4 tech × KYC'd users reached + ₹2 × 4.33 weekly debits). Cost to win = ₹450 × 1.1 contingency. Cumulative = −cost to win + Σ(revenue − serving); pays back in the first month it reaches zero.
These are the rules on the workbook's Engine, Products and Saver tabs, one column each.
