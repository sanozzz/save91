Pays back in month 75. LTV/CAC: what a saver brings in over 5 or 8 years, less the cost of serving them, divided by what it costs to win them.

WHERE ONE SAVER'S VALUE COMES FROM

Fund fees bring 74% of a saver's revenue in 5 years; cross-selling brings ₹143, less than the ₹190 it costs to serve the saver

Fund fees: 0.64% a year of the balance80% in equity funds at 0.75%, 20% in a debt fund at ~0.22%, paid while the saver keeps saving. A saver who stops takes the balance, so the fees stop.
Loans: about 11 per 100 savers in 5 yearsOne-off lender fees: ₹70k first loans at 2%; 20% of the users we can reach apply a year and 20% of first loans are approved (gold 70%, home and property 45%).
Serving: ₹190 in 5 years₹5 of support a month per saver still saving and ₹4 of tech for each of the 1.06 people reached, plus the 10% contingency. Debits ₹0 (to confirm).
One online saver won, the main case, undiscounted. Loans count the KYC'd sign-ups who came with the saver (KYC'd: finished the identity check). Cards: 3% of savers still saving at month 7 take one. Cost to win: ₹450 + 10% = ₹495; at ₹600 (₹660 with the contingency) a saver is about 0.56× / 0.99× and the company needs $12.0M+.

WHAT'S UNCERTAIN

What's still uncertain is mostly how long savers keep saving: 0.60–0.93× at 5 years, 1.00–1.76× at 8 (8 in 10 draws)

Two inputs are still uncertainStill saving at 12 months (Bachatt's 30–40%) and the debt fund's fee (0.10–0.35%). Everything else is fixed by the founders: market figures, Bachatt, or our judgement.
Retention decides itAt 30% still saving the median is 0.57×; at 40%, 0.98×. The debt fund's fee moves it only from 0.72× to 0.79×. In 8 in 10 draws a saver pays back from month 64 to not by month 96.
The company, in every drawEBITDA is not positive by month 96 in any of the 1,000 draws. 8 in 10 draws need $8.8M+ to $9.8M+: the company is still losing money at month 96.
The Monte Carlo: we ran the model 1,000 times, picking retention and the debt fund's fee at random within their ranges. 0.3% of the 1,000 draws reach 1× in 5 years, holding everything else at the main case (cost to win ₹495). The main case is the median draw; 8 in 10 draws fall between the 10th and 90th percentiles. Undiscounted.

WHAT IT TAKES: ONE LEVER AT A TIME

What it takes, one lever at a time: ₹130 a day or 40.5% still saving takes a saver to 1×; no loan number does on its own

A bigger daily amountFund fees are paid on the balance, so the daily amount moves LTV/CAC most: ₹130 a day pays back the ₹495 in 5 years; ₹200 gives 1.59×.
Longer saving40.5% still saving at 12 months gives 1× on its own; 45%, the credit hook we hope for (with 1.0% a month stopping after), 1.26×.
What cross-selling can doNo single loan number reaches 1×. All three at our old targets together give about 1.12× and bring profit in month 85: loan fees arrive early, fund fees build slowly.
Each row: one lever at its upside, everything else at the main case. Upside: ₹200 a day; 45% still saving; our old loan targets (applying 35%, approval 30%, take-up +5% and approval +10% a year). Alone for 1×: the value that lever needs on its own for a 5-year LTV/CAC of 1× (cost to win ₹495). Our old loan targets together: the company needs $6.0M.
