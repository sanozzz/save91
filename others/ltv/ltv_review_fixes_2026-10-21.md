# LTV/CAC deck: merged review fixes (21 Oct 2026)

Two fresh reviewers looked at the 28-page deck:
- **Numbers:** they recomputed every figure from the model and tables. All headline figures reproduce.
- **Story and layout:** 47 findings.

This is the one fix list. Items marked *not applied* say why.

## Numbers and wording that were wrong or misleading
1. p4 title: "about six times" by year 8 → "about six and a half times" (6.56× online, 6.50× doorstep).
2. p4: "after year 2 only savers remain" → "after year 1"; loan offers also keep reaching KYC'd users who never saved.
3. p1: "4 numbers decide it" → six (loan size, apply rate, cost to win, daily amount, payout, retention), matching p14.
4. p9: "these two are the most fragile" → only the apply rate (rank correlation 0.41, the highest). Reach is 8th.
5. Effect bars read backwards wherever a higher input lowers LTV/CAC (cost to win, withdrawals, taken back, support, late churn). The labels and the range column now follow the bar's left-to-right order.
6. p5: ₹600 is the seed funnel's budget, not a measurement; ₹450 is the founder's judgement. The page now shows what gets from ₹600 to ₹450: ₹70 a sign-up, or 1 in 3.75 saving.
7. p3: cross-sell is not "products savers choose". About ₹750 of the ₹1,845 LTV comes from loans to KYC'd users who never save; savers-only reach gives 2.4×. Said plainly.
8. p14: "every product moves LTV/CAC by under 0.15×" → 0.2× (family health cover swings 0.17×).
9. Loan counts 37 / 38 / 39 → one set: 32.5 first, 4.5 repeat, 1.4 top-ups = 38.4 per 100 savers, shown as 32 + 5 + 1 = 38. p9's bar is labelled "first + repeat".
10. Rounding footnotes on p3, p11 and p12 (₹191 vs ₹192, 315 vs 316).
11. p10: "₹38,36,963 lent" → "about ₹38 lakh".

## Honesty about assumptions (story reviewer)
12. p9: the nearest observed apply rate is 1–2% a year (merchants on a payments app). At 2%, LTV/CAC would be 1.6×, payback month 43. Said on the page and added to p16.
13. p10: the ₹1 lakh loan size and 30% first approval sit above the averages shown beside them. Said, with ₹50,000 → 3.1×.
14. p15: "our own ranges, not observed odds"; the cost to win is drawn from ₹300 to ₹600, so no future is worse than the seed's budget. p1 says "77% of our simulated futures".
15. p1, p17: only the per-saver answer recalculates live; the 1,000 futures and the one-at-a-time tests are rerun on request. "The arithmetic is checked; the assumptions are ours."
16. p16: pass lines for 3×, one input at a time:
    - at least 21% applying;
    - cost to win at most ₹615;
    - at least 27% still saving at a year;
    - loans of at least ₹47,000;
    - a payout of at least 0.94%;
    - offers reaching at least 1.5 users per saver.

    The scenario and "money needed" (peak cash before profit) are defined. The plan-of-record company figures are month 40 and $2.5M, so the reviewer's "month 70, $5.0M" memory is the superseded v3/v4 pre-marketplace plan.
17. p13: sign-ups who never save cost nothing to serve in this model. This is flagged as an assumption.
18. "The seed measures" → "we plan to measure"; distributor licence "to be registered"; the broker partner "to be signed".
19. "We assume…" leads on the p6, p7 and p9 titles; p7 "₹100 is a typical starting amount, not the floor".
20. p12: "where savings apps make their money" → "where we expect to earn on top".
21. One line on regulation (p9 and p16): rules on digital lending and referral payouts could change payouts.
