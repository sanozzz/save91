LTV / CAC, THE MAIN CASE

A saver won online costs ₹495 and returns ₹866 over five years in the main case: 1.75× LTV/CAC, paid back in month 39

1.75× in 5 years, 2.8× in 8LTV/CAC = what a saver brings in, less the cost of serving them, divided by the ₹495 it cost to win them. Undiscounted.
Loans are 59% of revenueLender fees of ₹745 on top of ₹433 of fund fees. Saving alone about covers its serving cost; the loans make the return.
At our targets: 7.7×Paid back in month 12 if every loan target holds at once. In the main case every input sits 48% of the way from today's market to its target.
Main case: every unmeasured input 48% of the way from today's market to our target, which gives the Monte Carlo median. ₹100 a day; 80/20 equity and debt funds. Serving = ₹5 support, ₹4 tech for each KYC'd user reached and ₹2 a weekly debit, a month while saving; the cost to win includes our 10% contingency. Not in this view: the company's fixed costs (team, office, brand).

THE ODDS

With the cost to win fixed at ₹495, the median saver returns 1.75×; 23% of the what-ifs reach 3× and 16% do not pay back in five years

Cost to win is known₹600 in the seed and ₹450 after, measured on our own funnel, plus our 10% contingency. It is held fixed; only the value of a saver is drawn.
The value is notEach of 10 unmeasured inputs is equally likely anywhere between today's market and our target. Loan inputs move together: savers who want credit apply more, borrow more and are approved more.
7.7× is the top cornerIt needs every input at its target at the same time. None of the 1,000 what-ifs gets there; the best tenth reach 4.0× or more.
A Monte Carlo: 1,000 random what-ifs, the loan inputs moving together. Market values: the middle of the 2024–26 evidence. LTV/CAC: revenue less serving over 60 months, divided by the cost to win; undiscounted.

At the median 1.75× EBITDA turns positive in month 63 on $4.7M; at 3×, month 46 on $3.1M; at our targets, month 33 on $1.8M

One plan, many outcomesEvery row is the same company: 20,000 online savers a month from month 30, the same team and the same cost to win. Only the value of a saver changes.
The main caseEvery unmeasured input 48% of the way from today's market to our target gives the median, 1.75×. The financials are built on it.
What the seed buysWhich row we are on. The next round (money needed less the $0.5M seed) is about $4.2M at the median and $2.6M at 3×.

THE FIVE NUMBERS

The $0.5M seed measures the five numbers that decide LTV/CAC, and with it whether the next round is about $4.2M or $2.6M

2Loan offers to KYC'd users who never saveTarget 1.5 more per saver; today's market savers only.Chance of 3×: 0% at market, 36% at target.
3Reachable users who apply for a loan, a yearTarget 35%; today's market 20%.Chance of 3×: 3% at market, 32% at target.
4Still saving after a year, and afterTarget 38%, then 1.5% stop a month; today's market 35%, then 2.5% stop a month.Chance of 3×: 16% at market, 30% at target.

HOW THE SEED GETS THERE
$0.5Mthe seed: build, licences, a lean team, the doorstep pilot
1 lakhsign-ups in months 4–18 to read the five numbers
Months 16–1812 months of data on the first savers; raise the next round (the seed budget runs to month 24 if it is late)
$4.2M or $2.6Mthe next round: in the main case, or if the seed proves 3×
The seed is not a profit plan. The cost to win is measured; the seed buys five measurements of what a saver is worth, and they decide the row of the ladder the next round is raised on.
Chance of 3×: the Monte Carlo with that one input fixed at today's market value, then at our target, every other input still drawn. Next round: money needed in all, less the $0.5M seed. The seed plan, a separate document, has the team and the use of funds.
