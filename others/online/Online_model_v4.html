Anyone can sign up savers; a product built for how they save and borrow is still missing, and the market is big enough for more than one winner

1
Sold on “believe me”
  • ✕An investment person they know picks the product. Nothing is written down.
  • ✓A written rule they can see: why this fund, in one sentence.
2
Can't start small alone
  • ✕A small instalment happens today because someone comes to collect it.
  • ✓₹100 a day by UPI Autopay, set up in minutes; an agent for 1 in 10.
3
Forgotten after joining
  • ✕Nobody comes back to ask what else the saver needs.
  • ✓Streaks, nudges and an AI money coach in the app.
4
Saves a little, then stops
  • ✕Some keep saving; many drop off.
  • ✓Saving improves the offer: the longer the streak, the better the loan limit and rate (our proposal).
What savers think the product is: a wallet they save into and then borrow from, an overdraft-like line. Our answer keeps the money in funds in the saver's own name (the app never holds it) and lets saving improve the loan offer.

THE MONEY FLOW

Money goes from the saver's bank to a fund in their name, never through us

In the saver's name, never through usUnits sit with the fund house, registered to the saver. The app never holds anyone's money.
One hybrid fund at ₹100 a dayDaily minimums are about ₹100 a scheme, so ₹80 equity + ₹20 debt likely becomes one hybrid fund. The model's 80/20 blend earns us 0.64% a year, so that fund's fee must be about 0.64% or more.
Paid by partnersFund houses and lenders pay us; the saver pays us nothing. A regular plan costs the saver about 0.5–1% a year more than a direct plan; we say so up front.
UPI Autopay mandate: a standing debit the saver approves once. SIP: a standing order to invest on a schedule. SEBI: payments come from the investor's own account. BSE StAR MF's exchange SIP is free to distributors (fund houses pay BSE); ₹0 a debit to us is not yet in writing (A16, page 29). NBFC = non-bank lender.
