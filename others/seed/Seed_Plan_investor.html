THE SEED'S JOB

The seed's job is to measure the five facts that decide the business

Two of them can each lift a saver past 1× on their own. Two are still unconfirmed and must be put in writing. No single loan number reaches 1×.

ONE SAVER, AT TODAY'S MARKET NUMBERS
Cross-selling alone (loans and cards): ₹143 per saver in 5 years, ₹173 in 8. That is 0.29× and 0.35× of the ₹495 it costs to win a saver, before serving, and less than the ₹190 / ₹225 it costs to serve them, so on its own it never pays back (−0.10× after serving). Insurance, recharge, bills, fixed deposits and pensions are not counted.
WITH FUND FEES, AFTER SERVING
0.75×in 5 years: ₹370 back on the ₹495 cost to win them
1.32×in 8 years: ₹655
Month 75when a saver has paid back the cost to win them
WHAT EACH FACT DOES TO ONE SAVER'S 5-YEAR RETURN, ONE AT A TIME
5-year return (LTV/CAC) = what a saver brings in over 5 years, less the cost of serving them, divided by what it costs to win them. Main case: inputs fixed by the founders on 24 October (market figures, Bachatt, or our judgement; appendix). We ran the model 1,000 times, picking retention (Bachatt's 30–40%) and the debt fund's fee at random within their ranges: 8 in 10 draws give 0.60×–0.93× at 5 years and 1.00×–1.76× at 8.

THE MONEY FLOW

Money moves from the saver's bank to the fund and back, never through us

In the saver's nameUnits sit with the fund house, registered to the saver.
Never through usWe hold no customer money, ever.
Paid by partnersFund houses and lenders pay us. A regular plan costs the saver about 0.5–1% a year more than a direct plan; we say so up front.
Sources: SEBI (payments come from the investor's own account); BSE StAR MF (no fee to distributors; ₹0 a debit to us, to confirm in writing); fund-house disclosures.
