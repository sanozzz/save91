The belief at the heart of the idea is that people who save with us repay better. If our losses stay at 4% a year or less, a shopkeeper pays back in 82 in 100 futures; if they reach the 7% or more that digital lenders see today, 49 in 100. The signs point the right way: repeat borrowers in your past lending went late only 1–2% of the time, and only 3.6% of loans above ₹50,000 ever go seriously late. But no Indian study shows that saving itself predicts repayment.
