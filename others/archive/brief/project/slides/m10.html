Four things could break it. One, winning shopkeepers may cost too much: it must stay under ₹704 a shopkeeper, and consumer apps pay about ₹499 per saver. Two, savers may not repay better than today's digital borrowers, who cost lenders 7–10% of the book a year. Three, an NBFC needs ₹10 crore of capital and ₹3–8 crore a year to run, and licences have taken up to about two years; co-lending with a bank needs far less. Four, the big payment apps and Bachatt already lend to these shopkeepers. A smaller fifth: the free fund route depends on fund houses continuing to pay the exchange.
