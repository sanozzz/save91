Save first, lend later · testing one premise · 12 October 2026

Can Paytm and PhonePe stop a newcomer from winning shopkeepers?

The premise. The plan wins shopkeepers with a daily savings habit, then lends to the ones who save well. That needs a newcomer to win, and lend to, shops that Paytm, PhonePe and BharatPe already serve. You put it as: "the QR code strategy is not defensible." This note tests that. It is not written to prove it.
Half true. The QR code itself protects no one: any shop can put up another, and most already have several. But the QR was never what protects Paytm and PhonePe. What protects them is that a shop's daily takings pass through their hands, so they take the loan instalment before the shop is paid. That part is hard to beat, and lending is exactly where we planned to make our money.

What it means for us. The idea does not stand or fall on the QR. It stands or falls on a narrower question nobody has answered yet:

Can a shopkeeper's saving record, plus our own way of collecting, lend as safely as a lender who sits on the till?

Until a pilot shows it can, shopkeepers are the hardest place to start, because that is where the till-holders are strongest. Pages 2–4 show the evidence; page 5 lists four ways the idea can live with the moat.

Where losses must land

Bad-loan losses a year, as a share of what is lent. One shopkeeper stops paying back for us above 7.6% (our break-even line from the deck).

The till-holders sit 2.6 points under our break-even. Lenders who must ask for their money, as we would, sit at or above it. The deck assumes 6%; this is why that number carries the most risk.

Two live tests

Two things have already happened that show which parts of the moat hold.

1. The 2024 shock: what happened when a QR giant was hit

In January 2024 the RBI ordered Paytm Payments Bank to stop most of its business, and Paytm had to move shops' payouts to other banks. It is the nearest thing to a live test of which layer holds.

What the test says.
  1. Which app customers pay with is not a moat. A new app can reach shopkeepers.
  2. The device and the daily payout are a moat. Shops stayed even while Paytm was in trouble.
  3. The lending is only as steady as the lenders behind it. Those lenders are partners anyone can approach, including us.

2. Khatabook: winning shops with a hook that never touches the till

Khatabook won shopkeepers with a free ledger app. Like savings, a ledger never touches the till. Then it found it "can't lend enough": loans too small, data unreliable, too little presence on the ground (Morning Context, 2023). FY24: revenue ₹102.7 crore, loss ₹116.2 crore. Winning shops with a hook worked; lending off the hook did not, yet.

Layer by layer

What Paytm, PhonePe and BharatPe actually hold, and how hard each piece is to take.

1

The QR code

✓Easy to copy
  • BharatPe launched the first QR any UPI app could pay in 2018. Since then a shop can add one more QR in a minute.
  • India has 56.9 crore QR codes for 6.5 crore shops: about 9 per shop (counts include inactive ones).
  • It earns little, so it locks in little: payments up to ₹2,000 stay free even after the 0.4% fee starts on 15 Oct 2026, and small shops are exempt.
  • BharatPe went from zero to 1.3 crore+ registered shops, and reached break-even in FY25 before employee-stock costs.
2

The soundbox and the people on the ground

!Sticky, but bought
  • Paytm has 1.57 crore shops paying for a device (+27 lakh in a year). PhonePe has 91.9 lakh speakers, 25,657 staff and 31,000+ agents.
  • Paytm spends up to about ₹5,000 of staff cost per device it adds (our estimate, an upper bound).
  • Cracks: shops left Paytm's ₹125-a-month box for PhonePe's ₹49 one; some hand the box back once rent starts; NPCI is reported to be building one box that announces payments from any QR (not confirmed).
3

Seeing the shop's takings

!Strong, but partial
  • Each company sees only what comes through its own QR. PhonePe reportedly lends only to shops taking ₹15,000+ a month on PhonePe QR for 3+ months.
  • With about 9 QR codes per shop, no one company sees the whole shop.
  • Account Aggregator, the RBI's consent system, lets any regulated lender read a shop's whole bank statement. Lending that used it reached ₹3.82 lakh crore in FY26 (8.4% of retail and small-business lending). It shows every QR's money, if the shopkeeper agrees.
4

Taking repayment from the till

✕Hard to beat
  • PhonePe takes a small instalment from the shop's daily payout. A missed one is added to the next day's; long misses are taken from the bank account. Paytm works the same way.
  • Result: Paytm-sourced shop loans expect 4.5%–5% losses, and more than half the money goes to repeat borrowers.
  • Without the till, we would collect by UPI Autopay. In Aug 2025, 55%–90% of Autopay debits failed at the big banks, mostly for lack of money.
5

Lending to shops at scale

✕Already there
  • PhonePe's shop loans grew from ₹11 crore (FY23) to ₹4,507 crore (FY25), and ₹3,820 crore in Apr–Sep 2025 alone, 2.1× a year earlier.
  • BharatPe earns 60% of its revenue from financial services, mostly shop loans of about ₹1 lakh over 11 months. Google Pay lends to shops through partners too.
  • Paytm and PhonePe do not lend their own money: Paytm will not seek a lending licence, and PhonePe has applied three times since 2021. BharatPe lends 25–30% through its own lending company. The lenders behind them are partners we could also approach.
6

Our savings hook

✕Already copied
  • PhonePe Daily Recurring Deposit: ₹100–1,000 a day, withdraw after 7 days, with Shivalik Small Finance Bank (6 Aug 2026, for consumers).
  • PhonePe has offered daily saving in gold with Jar since 2024. BharatPe sells deposits to 80 lakh+ shops through its investment platform.
  • A shop version is a small step for any of them. So savings can open the door, but it is not an edge they cannot copy.

For shopkeepers, the premise that matters is losses, not the QR.

How Bachatt lives without the till

Bachatt already sells daily saving to this customer, and investors have put about $16 million into it. It does not sit on the counter, and it does not hold the loan. Two different filters are easy to mix up.

  1. Who shows up is a filter, and it is small. Bachatt claims 3 million users since May 2025. In February 2026 it ran 2 million fund purchases. If one daily saver produces about 20 purchases a month, that is about 1 lakh people still saving: 3.3% of the people who signed up. The other 97% never become a lending problem. Our model already works this way. The cost to win and the value per shopkeeper count the person we got, not every shop on the street.
  2. Who repays is a second filter, and nobody has shown it. Partners book Bachatt's loans, up to ₹5 lakh, so a 7%–10% loss sits on the partner, not on Bachatt. We said we would hold the loan. Holding it, and changing only the loss rate, one shopkeeper is worth ₹597 at 3% losses, ₹205 at the 6% the model assumes, and −₹319 at 10%. The 3% is the hope that people who choose to save are safer. It is not the base case.

Bachatt has published no retention, loan volume, or default figure. The funding is a bet that the second filter is real. It is not evidence that it is.

Four ways the idea can live with the moat

A. Go where no one holds the tillSalaried workers through employers: the salary deduction becomes our till. In our model, at 24% a salaried customer is worth +₹748 in the low-loss case (3% losses), but −₹153 in the normal case (6%). So it works only if deduction keeps losses low.
B. Ask for the whole bank statementMake Account Aggregator consent part of joining. We would then see money from all of a shop's QR codes, where Paytm sees only its own.
C. Let the savings secure the loanA year of ₹100 a day is ₹36,500, about 24% of a ₹1.5 lakh loan, if every debit succeeds. Lending against the saver's own fund units would make each loss smaller.
D. Partner instead of fightingBe the savings product inside BharatPe's or PhonePe's app. Shops become cheap to reach, but the partner keeps the customer and the lending, which removes the business we modelled.
What the pilot must add to the four numbers it already measures:
  1. How many of our savers already have a Paytm, PhonePe or BharatPe loan.
  2. How many will share their bank statement through Account Aggregator.
  3. What share of daily Autopay loan instalments succeed (not savings debits).
  4. Whether shopkeepers keep saving with us when PhonePe offers ₹100 a day at the same counter.
