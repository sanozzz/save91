Say: The second catch. Loans are offered from month 4, but by month 3 only 50% of savers are still saving, 22% after a year and 12% after two, which is what the founder expects for these workers. So in the middle case only 1 in 16, 6.4%, ever takes a loan within five years. If loans had to pay for finding savers, each borrower would cost ₹4,006: ₹256 per saver divided by 6.4%. Lending first costs ₹760. So the flip does not make borrowers cheaper; it makes savers cheaper, and savers only pay if they also buy gold and insurance and stay. Even in the best case, 1 in 5 borrow and a borrower costs ₹1,061, against ₹480 lending first. Likely question: Couldn't you offer loans sooner? Answer: Yes, from month 3 in the best case, but then you are lending on three months of data, which is close to lending first.
