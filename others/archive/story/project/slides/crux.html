Say: Put the two catches together and the story rests on three things. One: savers stay; 22% still saving after a year in the middle case, 36% needed. Two: debits get cheap; ₹2 a debit today, ₹0.50 needed. Three: what we sell pays; 9% of active savers have borrowed by month 12 in the middle case, 23% needed, with insurance at 16% against 26%, and gold, loan and insurance fees at their best-case rates. With all three, each saver earns ₹1,123 over five years, 5.3 times what it cost to win them, and revenue reaches about $115M in year 10. If only the third goes right, it still doesn't work; if everything goes right except the third, it falls to 1.0. Missing cheap debits or savers staying costs about two points each (3.2 and 3.3), which still clears 3. Two smaller assumptions, how much people save and what they cost to win, matter less. Likely question: Isn't 'all three at once' wishful? Answer: Yes. That is why no money goes past the pilot until the three readings come in.
