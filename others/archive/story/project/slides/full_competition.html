Say: Jar owns daily gold saving: 35M registered users, but a ₹50.5 cr loss in FY25 even after two profitable quarters. Bachatt is aimed squarely at our self-employed half: daily fund saving for merchants earning ₹30,000–70,000 a month (its stated target); it already sells loans and cards, and Accel, Lightspeed and Info Edge put $12M into it in March 2026. Payment apps own the shopkeeper relationship: PhonePe alone has 4.72 crore registered merchants, and India has about 6.5 crore UPI merchants. PhonePe already offers daily gold saving built with Jar, and Paytm and Google Pay offer gold and FDs. So the saving habit is not a moat. The only defensible edge would be credit: lending to savers better than a loan app can, because we see the saving streak and, with a platform partner, the payout record. Research shows delivery-app data predicts creditworthiness for people with no credit history (RappiCard, Mexico); no study yet shows the same for saving in India. Likely question: What stops Bachatt from targeting riders? Answer: Nothing. Speed and a platform partnership are the only answers, and neither is a moat.
