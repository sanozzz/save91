Why save first

Lending first turns most people away and needs capital; saving first turns nobody away and needs almost none.

Say: Lending first, you pay to find every applicant and then turn most away: only 25% were approved in the founder's past lending data, so each borrower cost ₹760 to find. Someone also has to fund the loans: ₹0.84 of capital for every rupee of revenue. And covering all of a lender's losses, the old way to make this work, is now barred: an app may cover at most 5%. Saving first, nobody is turned away. A saver costs ₹256 to win, and because partners hold both the money and the loans, we lock up only ₹0.09 per rupee of revenue, the 5% loss cover. The part nobody has proven is that savers repay better. The model assumes it for gig workers, 4.5% of loans 90+ days late against 6.5%, but assumes 7% for the self-employed, who are riskier. Likely question: Is a saver worth the same as a borrower? Answer: No, and the next two slides are about exactly that.
