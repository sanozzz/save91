Say: Here is the honest comparison. Save-first costs ₹256 to win a saver who makes a first debit; lend-first cost ₹760 to win a borrower. Neither earns back its CAC within five years in the base case. The saver brings −₹258 of discounted profit, a loss, because serving a daily saver costs more than the base-case cross-sell earns. The borrower brings ₹54. After paying to win them, save-first is at −₹514 a customer and lend-first at −₹706. The real difference is capital and legality. Save-first locks ₹0.09 of loss-cover deposit per rupee of revenue; lend-first needs ₹0.84 of equity in our own NBFC, because the way Bueno ran it is now barred. In the best case save-first reaches 5.3 times CAC against 2.3. Likely question: So why flip at all? Answer: The flip is legal, needs little capital and has the higher ceiling.
