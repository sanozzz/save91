Say: How each works. Savings buddy: two friends share a goal and see each other's streaks. Rider hubs: field agents with QR codes sign riders up between shifts. Payout day: the rider's own mandate debits right after the platform payout lands. Associations: sessions at trader associations, cooperatives and self-help groups. UPI sales: a mandate timed after the shop's daily UPI collections land. Suppliers: distributors who visit shops weekly introduce us. These are costs per saver who completes a first auto-debit, fully loaded: field staff or media, KYC at about ₹40, the mandate, and any partner fee. For gig workers: savings buddy ₹73, rider hubs ₹213, saving on payout day ₹220. For the self-employed: trade associations and self-help groups ₹185, a mandate timed after the shop's daily UPI sales ₹243, and supplier networks ₹274; market field agents cost ₹302. Paid ads cost ₹750. Two legal limits shape the self-employed channels: a fund distributor may share commission only with registered sub-distributors, and nobody but a licensed intermediary may be paid for insurance leads. So suppliers and associations can be paid only for gold referrals, or not at all. No payments firm publishes its merchant acquisition cost, so the self-employed channel costs are the least certain numbers in the model; the base case already adds half again to the acquisition part. Likely question: Why not lucky draws or cashback? Answer: A non-bank may not run prize draws, and cashback is barred on fund saving.
