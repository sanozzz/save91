# Independent investment review: save91

**Date:** 18 October 2026
**Scope:** Current base only. Source of truth is `tables/base_*.csv`, the one-agent cohort tables, `tables/dist_agreed_v1.csv`, the research rows those inputs cite, and `Offline_model_v3.pdf`. Superseded files were used only to check that the current files do not silently reuse them. `Cohort_model.xlsx` was ignored. The Google Sheet was not available.

**How this was checked.** Year columns, the P&L ladder, the cash roll, the cap table and the NBFC identities were recomputed from the published tables. The model was not re-run. Each city's cap was not re-simulated. The company-level Monte Carlo draws are not saved, so the depth of the cash-failure tail is unknown.

**Units.** ₹ crore and lakh (1 crore = 100 lakh = 10 million). The founder uses ₹100 = $1, so ₹10,000 crore is the unicorn line. Months are counted from company launch. Y5 is months 49–60.

**Kinds of finding.** (a) arithmetic, or a label that contradicts the file. (b) a modelling choice. (c) a judgement input. "Verified" means recomputed from the current tables or read off the cited row. "Suspected" means the direction is clear and the rupee impact was not re-run through the engine.

## 1. Executive summary

- The base is internally consistent on the arithmetic. Year columns equal the monthly sums. The ladder revenue → CM1 → CM2 → CM3 → EBITDA → EBIT → PBT → PAT holds. Cash rolls forward, and the low is ₹8.95 crore in month 8, never negative. The cap table, including the ESOP top-up, matches `base_rounds.csv` on the printed decimals. NBFC interest, provisions, borrowings and equity reconcile, and the equity in the loan book is not also sitting in group cash.
- The decision breaks on the valuation. "Unicorn on paper from month 59" is a 16-month window. At month 60 the AUM lens clears ₹10,000 crore by 0.7%, the median draw misses it (median lens 2 is ₹9,366 crore; both lenses clear in 29% of draws), and 20× ARR is 0.49× AUM, so the two headline lenses are almost one test. Lens 3, the only one that keeps the NBFC separate, is ₹6,890 crore.
- Year-5 lending revenue is built on a ₹2.5 lakh personal loan and a ₹25 lakh home loan. The folder's own segment evidence is about ₹25,600 and ₹58,522 for personal loans, and a housing ATS of ₹11.8 lakh. The 18% yearly apply rate uses a lifetime figure the note itself says is not a yearly rate. The best 30% of personal loans are kept on book; the partners' 70% keep the full, rising DSA fee.
- The AUM is filled by a cash route the folder's own SEBI and AMFI notes disallow, at 100 sign-ups a month taken from one LinkedIn chat and at 55% of a cohort still saving at month 12. The deck's own "24 in 100 still saving" is the online evidence, and the only field interview in the folder is a book of 200–400 with 6–10 new customers a month.
- Year-5 PAT of ₹17 crore is ₹25.5 crore of interest on raised cash against EBITDA of −₹7.9 crore. Cumulative EBITDA through month 60 is −₹368 crore. I would not lead, and I would not join a round priced off either headline lens, until a pilot has measured sign-ups while collecting, month-12 survival, and a real lender's ticket and fee.

## 3. Ten diligence questions

1. Which named lender has approved, in writing, a ticket and a fee for a saver whose income record is a cash deposit, after being told the NBFC keeps the better 30%?
2. Who has title to a rupee between the shop counter and the AMC collection account, and which counsel has said this is neither an unregulated deposit nor a breach of the own-account rule?
3. In the pilot, what are sign-ups per agent in month 3, counted only after the time spent collecting from the existing book, set next to the janta agent's 6–10 a month?
4. What share of the first cohort is still saving in month 12, reported separately from the book ratio, and what share of the rupees arrived by UPI from the saver's own account?
5. If the AUM lens is dropped and net interest is taken out of the ARR multiple, which round is actually being raised, and at what pre-money?
6. Both lenses are under ₹10,000 crore again by year 7 on the model's own table, and savers fall from about 37 lakh to 24 lakh by year 8. What is the plan for the years after the cities fill?
7. Who services and collects a ₹1,576 crore unsecured book on about ₹3 crore of NBFC operating cost, and what is the credit cost if the kept 30% looks like the CRIF self-employed 90+ curve?
8. Reprice rounds C and E so pre-money is no higher than the previous post-money and no higher than the tighter lens. Seed, A and B are already at the 25% cap. How much less is raised, and in which month does cash go negative?
9. What happens to the 30% a year agent attrition when the balance incentive is cut from 0.25% to 0.10% at month 60, and when each replacement takes 10% of the inherited book?
10. What share of the people actually signed can deposit ₹7,000 a month and also pay a ₹10,000 EMI? Split the IV-04 customer (₹20–40k) from the NG-045 vendor (₹9–15k).

## 4. Investment view

I would not lead the next round on these numbers, and I would not join a round priced off either headline lens.

The work in the folder is careful in a way that most early models are not. The ladder, the cap table, the city ceiling and the NBFC identities tie, and several of the soft spots are disclosed: the city cap, the shrinkage after year 5, the 0.7% AUM-lens margin, the single chat behind the sign-up rate, the cash-route question, and the fact that Paytm's ₹407 is a different business. Disclosure is worth something. It does not make the headlines usable. Year-5 profit is the interest on money that has been raised. The unicorn is a 16-month overlap of two multiples that are, on this revenue mix, the same multiple applied twice, and the median of the founder's own draws does not get there.

Three things would have to be true before a price. The cash route would need a clean legal opinion, or it would need to be rebuilt so the debit is from the saver's own account or a bank business correspondent takes the deposit. A lender would need to have signed a term sheet at a stated ticket, a stated fee and a stated rejection rate, knowing the NBFC keeps the better 30%. And a six-to-twelve-month pilot would need to have measured what one agent can sign while he is also collecting, and what share of that first cohort is still saving at month 12.

The three numbers the pilot would have to prove, in this order, are sign-ups per agent per month net of collection time, the month-12 still-saving rate of the first cohort with the UPI share of rupees beside it, and the loan size and fee a real lender will approve on the book that lender is actually left with.

Until those exist, underwrite year-5 EBITDA of −₹8 crore and cumulative EBITDA through month 60 of −₹368 crore, ignore PAT, ignore 0.45× AUM, and treat lens 3 — ₹6,890 crore at month 60, short of ₹10,000 crore, and above it in 12% of the draws — as the only valuation arithmetic in the folder that separates a fee business from a leveraged loan book.
