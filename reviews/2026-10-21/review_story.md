# Story review: Save91 / SaveMore investor materials (plan of record 21 Oct 2026)

Reviewer angle: an early-stage VC and pitch coach reading cold. I read all five HTML sources and checked layout in the PDFs.
Page numbers are PDF pages. "v4" is `Online_model_v4`, "BC" is `Online_business_case`, "LTV" is `LTV_CAC_deck` and "Seed" is `Seed_Plan_investor`.

---

## 1. The story in one sentence, as a partner would retell it

| Document | What a partner says afterwards |
|---|---|
| **v4** | "A savings app for the self-employed that really earns by referring loans: 4.1× LTV/CAC, profit in year 4 on $2.5M, or bigger on $10.4M. $500k to find out if people borrow." |
| **BC** | "Same numbers, but the cover says $1B by year 5, and the $1B is only in the good scenarios." |
| **LTV** | "Careful work, but it rests on 35% of KYC'd users applying for a loan every year. Their own page says the nearest observed rate is 1–2%, and at 2% it's 1.6×." |
| **Seed** | "$500k, five people, 24 months, a third held back. I don't know who the founders are." |

**After 3 minutes** the partner remembers three numbers (4.1×, month 40, $0.5M) and one word: loans.
**After 30 minutes** they remember four doubts:
- the loan apply rate;
- that about 40% of a saver's value comes from people who never save;
- that the only $1B plan is one the deck itself calls impossible;
- that there is no team, market or competition page.

**Is it the story the founders want to tell?** No. Their sharpest thinking is in the internal Bachatt memo:
- "Savings is the hook; credit is the business."
- "Committed savers + their savings record → credit quality an app built for sign-ups can't match."

Neither sentence appears in any investor document. The four documents agree on the numbers, but none of them states the insight or the edge. The investor decks tell a spreadsheet story; the memo tells a company story.

## 2. P1 findings: these break the story

### P1-1. The product story and the model say opposite things about credit
*v4 p1, p2, p6; LTV p3*

- The cover promises "credit as the reward for saving".
- v4 p2: "Credit tied to the streak: save 6 months, see your loan offer (our proposal)."
- v4 p6: "Every KYC'd user, from day 1… 2.5 people see loan offers for every saver won."
- LTV p3: "About ₹750 of the ₹1,845 comes from loans to KYC'd sign-ups who never save; with offers to savers only, LTV/CAC is 2.4×."

So the economics come from marketing loans to people who did not save, which is the opposite of the cover. A diligent partner finds this on LTV p3. They then either decide the story is decoration or that the founders have not seen the gap.

**Fix:** say plainly:
> "We win and keep customers with daily saving; we earn when they borrow. The base case is a plain referral business that works without our edge. Credit priced from the savings record is the upside the seed tests."

That makes the "thin-fee lead business" (the memo's phrase) a floor rather than a hidden truth. Then drop "save 6 months, see your loan offer", or model it.

### P1-2. "Nobody serves both" is contradicted by the team's own memo, and there is no competition page
*v4 p2*

The v4 p2 headline reads: "They will save a small amount every day and want to borrow when they need to; nobody serves both."

The memo shows that Bachatt does exactly this: daily savings plus DSA loans, backed by about $16M from Accel. Jar has 35M registered users, is profitable, and runs ₹10-a-day saving inside PhonePe.

No investor document has a competition page, and Jar is never mentioned. Any Indian fintech partner will know both companies, so this headline costs credibility on page 2.

**Fix:** use the real gap: "Others sign up savers; nobody keeps them long enough to lend on their record." Add the memo's map plus "where we fight" as one page.

### P1-3. Too many cases: the founders' "one case" rule is broken
*v4 p1, p7–p11; BC p1*

Before the ask, v4 shows:
- two plans on the cover ("Month 40… the profitable path" and "Month 39 growing fast");
- 12 one-at-a-time cases (p7);
- 6 scales (p8);
- 3 plans (p9);
- 6 combinations at a fourth scale, 2.5 lakh a month, which matches none of the three named plans (p10);
- 1,000 futures with P10/P50/P90 (p11).

BC's cover leads with "$1B by year 5: 3 of 5 combinations clear $1B… the cheapest needs $11.5M". That is a scenario, not the plan.

The partner cannot answer "what is the plan?" without help.

**Fix:** one plan of record in the story, and one sentence for the scale choice ("the seed decides whether we raise $2.0M for the profitable path or $9.9M to grow 7× faster"). Move p8–p11 to the appendix or cut them.

### P1-4. The base case is labelled "today's evidence" but rests on judgement
*v4 p1, p7, p8, p11; BC p1*

"Today's evidence" appears 20 times across v4 and BC. LTV p9 describes the inputs behind it:
- the apply rate is "Founder's judgement; no observed rate";
- the ₹450 cost to win is the founder's judgement;
- 38% retention is "an extrapolation".

On v4 p7, "₹600 to win a saver (our funnel today)" gives 3.1×. So the deck has two "todays" that disagree.

v4 p11 then claims "98% chance of profit by month 60". LTV p15 rightly calls the same simulation "our own ranges, not observed odds: a stress test, not a forecast", but v4 drops that caveat, and the apply-rate range (10–50%) excludes the only observed rate (1–2%).

**Fix:** rename the base case "the plan", delete "98% chance", and point to the pass lines (P1-7).

### P1-5. The deck undercuts its own venture scale
*v4 p9, p10*

- The plan of record reaches ₹77 Cr revenue in year 8 and fails $1B on both yardsticks (p9: "no"). Grow fast also fails.
- The only $1B plan, "Capture 10 crore", carries this note on the same page: "needs 46 crore sign-ups, more than the segment: it works only if about 1 in 3 start saving."
- The same page adds: "Team costs… are likely understated."

A page that proposes a plan and then refutes it tells a VC the founders do not believe their own upside. p10 also leans on "0.45× money saved", which the 18 Oct review flagged as a multiple on money the company does not own.

**Fix:** cut the 10-crore column and the unicorn page. Make the upside credible instead: the same machine at 1.5 lakh savers a month, plus the credit edge, plus later the company's own lending licence (which p18 already lists).

### P1-6. The standard investor pages are missing
*all documents*

- **Team:** Seed p6 is headed "Five people, and $92k of salaries over 24 months". It is a cost table with first names only. The best "why us" fact sits in a footnote: one founder ran a lending business (v4 p28: "Founder's past lending data… 2019–2022").
- **Market:** there is no market page. The pool ("about 7 crore urban self-employed") appears only in BC's appendix and in a v4 footnote that calls it "our agreed pool".
- **Why now:** never stated, though the folder has every fact needed:
  - UPI Autopay daily mandates;
  - SEBI's ₹250 small SIP;
  - the RBI's 2025 digital-lending rules;
  - a funded category.
- **Evidence:** the customer problem is sourced to "practitioner conversations, 17–18 Oct 2026 (…the wallet reading is the founders' own)". That is not customers.
- **The ask:** v4 p12 says "The seed plan, a separate document, has the team and the use of funds." A main deck without the team or the ask is not a pitch deck.

### P1-7. The seed milestones are profit months, not round-ready targets
*v4 p12; Seed p8*

The "five numbers" are described by their effect on a profit month three or four years out: "45%: month 39"; "₹200 brings profit 1 months sooner".

A seed investor wants to know what will be true at month 18. LTV p16 already has the answer ("Where 3× holds"):
- at least 21% apply a year (plan 35%);
- a cost to win of at most ₹615;
- at least 27 in 100 still saving at a year;
- loans of at least ₹47,000;
- a payout of at least 0.94%;
- offers reaching at least 1.5 users per saver.

These pass lines show the room in the plan, which answers the partner's biggest worry.

**Fix:** put them on v4 p12 and Seed p8, and add "a signed lender at a stated payout and ticket size". Also reconcile the lists: the seed's five numbers include the doorstep arm, while LTV's "6 numbers" (p1, p14) include loan size and payout.

## 3. P2 findings: these confuse or cost credibility

**P2-1. Headline numbers that differ between documents.**

| Item | Where | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Retention downside (24%) | v4 p7, BC p3 / LTV p6, p14 | 2.6× / 2.7× (v4 also lowers month-3 retention; footnote only) |
| Doorstep saver value | v4 p12 / LTV p1 / LTV p3 | ₹1,717 / ₹1,550 / ₹1,145: revenue, after serving and net, all unlabelled |
| Cost to win | LTV p3 / v4 p16 | ₹450 / "₹450… costs ₹495 in the company model". A partner gets 1,845 ÷ 495 = 3.7×. |
| Profit month | v4 p1 / v4 p8 / BC p1 | "Month 40" / "months 39–42" / "Year 4" |
| Length of the seed | Seed p1 / v4 p16, LTV p2 | 24 months / "the first 18 months" |
| Loans per 100 savers | v4 p6 title / v4 p6 chart | 73 / chart ends at 37 |
| Doorstep share | v4 p3 / Seed p7 | 1 in 10 / "13% from the doorstep" |
| Plan vs simulated median | LTV p1, p15 | 4.1× vs 3.8× ("ranges are wider on the downside"): the base case sits above the deck's own median |

**P2-2. The founders' cumulative P&L rule is not met** (v4 p8, p19).
No document states cumulative EBITDA. From p19 it is −₹14.7 Cr by year 3 and +₹5.9 Cr by year 5. "Money needed" adds "a ₹5 crore floor" to peak cash, so it is not plain peak cash. Show both on one line.

**P2-3. The loan funnel chart does not read as a funnel** (v4 p6, LTV p9).
- The bars go 500 → 250 → 95 → **105** → 37, mixing a stock with five-year flows.
- "₹98,274" average loan is false precision.
- The deck never says plainly that four non-savers sign up for every saver, and half of them still get loan offers.

**P2-4. "We charge the saver nothing"** (v4 p4, Seed p4).
The trail comes from the regular-plan fee, which costs the saver about 0.5–1% a year more than a direct plan. A partner will call it spin. Say instead: "the fund house pays us out of its published fee; the saver pays no fee to us."

**P2-5. 80% equity for an emergency saver is not addressed** (v4 p3).
The source is "practitioner conversation; not yet filed", and debt-only gives 3.5×. Expect questions on suitability and on what a market fall does to retention.

**P2-6. The doorstep arm has no stated hypothesis** (v4 p12, p22).
- p12: "Without it: profit in the same month (40)." Online-only even needs $0.2M less.
- The reason to run it (does a visit lift retention and credit quality?) is only in the memo.
- p3 says agents "never handle cash", while p18 lists a legal opinion on "any cash at the doorstep".

**P2-7. Appendix pages that should not travel to investors** (v4 p23–p26).
- A10 says "8 wrong or unsourced… Rebuilt 21 Oct".
- A11 says "118 of 120 checks within target".
- A12 quotes the old plan's "₹1,108 crore over six rounds; unicorn on paper by month 59".

These tell a partner that the plan changed this week and was still being corrected on its date. Cut A10–A13; A13 repeats A8.

**P2-8. Internal shorthand.**
- "Agreed" appears 20 times in v4 ("agreed pool", "Agreed street input"), with no "by whom".
- Also internal: "hazards", "Latin hypercube", "PERT draws", "rank correlation", "P10/P50/P90", "pool cap", "Business model v4", and "Online model v4" in every footer.
- LTV carries about 75 research IDs ("XF-24", "NG-033") and "Founder's judgement, 21 Oct" in its body.
- v4 p7's "Credit-hungry: 45% stay + 50% apply" is two changes on a page titled "one change at a time".

**P2-9. Titles carry three numbers, not one point.**
v4 p10's title is 30 words long, and p7 and p8 have the same problem. Use one point per title and put the numbers in the body.

**P2-10. Layout faults.**
- Seed p8: the milestone cards overflow into the footer.
- BC p6: the year labels overlap the footnote and legend, and the "Today's evidence" row has no bar.

**P2-11. BC repeats v4 and leads with the weakest claim.**
Six of its nine pages are v4 pages. It has no customer or product page, and its cover leads with unicorn combinations. Retire it, or rebuild it as a two-page summary.

**P2-12. Go-to-market is generic.**
"App, referrals and performance ads" (v4 p3) is all there is. There is no first city or segment (the memo leaves "shopkeepers and vendors first?" open), and nothing on how an ad reaches the self-employed in particular.

**P2-13. The 32% contingency invites "so you need $338k?"** (Seed p1).
Call it runway: an 18-month plan plus 6 months to close the next round.

## 4. P3 findings: polish
- "Working name, to be finalised" is on every page, under two names. Pick one first.
- Seed p8: "1 months" (twice).
- Seed p6: "$100k cap" is not explained.
- v4 p1: "7× the active savers". Seven times what?
- The negative cost-to-win bar is teal, the colour used elsewhere for good outcomes.
- Loans are one bar in v4 p5 but two in LTV p3. Use one breakdown.
- "Cross-sell is 73% of revenue" hides the plainer point: loans are 63%.
- Bachatt is named on v4 p14 and BC p9 but is "a competitor" elsewhere. Be consistent; I would name competitors and keep the conversation source private.
- Memo (internal): "Mutual fund (in his name)" is gendered, and its sources table identifies a private conversation with a competitor's growth head. Strip both if it is ever shared.
- The investor decks are otherwise gender-neutral. I found no customer names, phone numbers or agent-network partners.

## 6. Recommended storyline for the main deck (13 pages)

| # | Page | The one point |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | Daily saving that turns into credit for India's 7 crore self-employed; raising $0.5M. Three numbers: 4.1× (paid back in month 19), profit from month 40 on $2.5M in all, and what $0.5M buys. |
| 2 | The saver | One person, gender-neutral: saves cash at home, borrows from a moneylender, and has no record a lender can see. |
| 3 | Insight | Saving wins and keeps the customer; credit is where the money is. A daily savings record is a credit signal nobody owns yet. |
| 4 | Product | ₹100 a day by UPI Autopay into funds in the saver's own name → streak → offers. Six screens (Seed p3) and four releases. |
| 5 | Money flow | We never hold money; partners pay us; no RBI licence on day one (v4 p4). |
| 6 | Why now | Daily UPI mandates, the ₹250 SIP, the 2025 lending rules, a funded category. |
| 7 | Competition | The map, and where we fight: keeping savers and pricing credit from their record. |
| 8 | One saver | The waterfall plus payback curve; loans are 63%; 2.5 KYC'd users are reached per saver, stated plainly. |
| 9 | What has to be true | LTV p16's six pass lines against the plan ("21% must apply; we plan 35%"). |
| 10 | Saver to company | One plan: cumulative EBITDA (years 3 and 5), profit month, peak cash, revenue in years 5 and 8. One sentence on the scale lever. |
| 11 | Go-to-market | Seed funnel (1 lakh sign-ups → 22,200 savers), first city and segment, the doorstep test and its hypothesis. |
| 12 | Team | Backgrounds, the lending track record, the 3 hires. |
| 13 | The ask | $0.5M, 24-month runway, use of funds, and the month-18 targets that size a $2.0M or $9.9M next round. |

Appendix: inputs and ranges, regulatory path, year-by-year tables for the plan, sensitivity, simulated futures (with LTV's "stress test" caveat), and loan sources.

Keep the LTV deck as the diligence pack, after three changes:
- strip the research IDs and the dates;
- reconcile its numbers with the main deck (P2-1);
- move its page 16 into the main deck.

The seed plan becomes a budget appendix once its team page answers "why us" and its milestone cards are fixed.
