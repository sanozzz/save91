# The customer's problem

This is the page the deck does not have. The published deck opens on our cost of winning him online (₹499, and 24 in 100 still saving). That is our problem. His is below. The words are the ones to put on the page, before Part 1.

## What is wrong for him

1. **He is sold on "believe me".** An investment person he knows picks the product. Nothing is written down. He cannot see the rule that chose it.
2. **He cannot start a small SIP himself.** A small instalment happens today because a person comes to the shop and collects it.
3. **Once the money is in, he is forgotten.** Nobody comes back to ask what else he needs.

## What the current base does with each

| Problem | What the base does | What is still open |
|---|---|---|
| "Believe me" | Nothing. There is no suitability record. From month 1 the marketplace sells loans, cards and insurance. | The page has to say what replaces the person's word: a written rule he can see. |
| Small instalment | An agent collects about ₹350 a day in cash, every 2–3 days. | A conversation with Bachatt says collection there is 100% UPI, and that people choose an equity SIP. Those two answers to this problem are different products. The deck has to pick one. The only small-SIP scheme in the folder (₹250 a month, XO-01) is equity or hybrid. A debt fund cannot use it. |
| Forgotten after the money is in | The agent visits every 2–3 days. The visit exists to take cash. | A needs visit is not costed separately. If collection moves to a UPI mandate, this visit goes away, and the marketplace has no one whose job is to come back. |

## What the pilot has to show

- That a saver can be told, in one sentence, why this product was chosen for him.
- Whether the small instalment is a UPI mandate he starts, or cash a person collects. Not both as the base.
- That someone comes back after the first month for a reason other than taking the instalment, and what that visit costs.
