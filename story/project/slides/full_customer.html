Say: Half our savers are app delivery and ride workers. The best data is IDinsight's 2024 survey of 2,547 riders on one platform across India: a full-time rider nets ₹18,761 after fuel and phone, lasts about 13 months on a platform, and half of those who owned a bike or phone borrowed to buy it. The other half are the self-employed: shopkeepers, tradespeople like electricians and tailors, auto and car drivers outside the apps, domestic workers and small business owners. They make up 39.1% of Delhi's workers. The urban self-employed average ₹23,012 a month (₹34,868 in Delhi) in the government's labour survey, but that average includes shop owners with staff, and the survey does not say whether it is before or after business costs, so it cannot be compared cleanly with a rider's ₹18,761 net or ₹27,814 gross. Street vendors mostly earn ₹9,000–15,000. The only head-to-head study, from Kenya, found market vendors saved about 2.2x as much as bike-taxi drivers. Likely question: Why two customers at once? Answer: The self-employed stay longer and save more, which raises the best case; they are also riskier borrowers. The pilot reads each half separately.
