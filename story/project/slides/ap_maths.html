A

Every number here is on the Outputs tab of savings_first_model.xlsx, in the story block, and the workbook's formulas are checked against a separate Python engine on 1,989 values across 9 cases. The cost per saver who ever borrows is the cost to win a saver divided by the share of activated savers who take a first loan within 60 months. It overstates what a borrower costs, because savers also bring gold and insurance revenue; it is there to show that saving first does not make borrowers cheaper to find.
