How it dies

Four ways it dies, and we would see each one within the first year.

Before launch

Debits stay expensive

Payment quotes stay above ₹0.50 a debit; the list price is ₹5.

Then: Switch to one debit a week.

Month 3

Savers stop

Fewer than 50% of savers are still saving.

Then: Stop paying for sign-ups; fix the product first.

Months 6 to 12

Savers don't buy

Only about 9% of active savers have borrowed by month 12.

Then: Stop: what is left is a thin gold margin.

Any time

Gold rules tighten

Gold becomes a regulated security, or its margin is cut. Gold is 39% of revenue over five years.

Then: Move daily saving into ₹100+ fund debits.

A fifth risk is outside our control: a big app does it first. PhonePe already offers daily gold with Jar.

Say: Assume this failed. There are four likely ways, and each shows up early. Before launch: if payment companies won't quote ₹0.50 a debit or less, against a list price of ₹5, daily saving can't pay, so we switch to weekly. Month 3: if fewer than 50% are still saving, we are below even the middle case, and we stop spending on sign-ups. Months 6 to 12: if only about 9% of active savers have borrowed by month 12, the middle case, the business is a thin gold margin and we stop. Any time: gold is unregulated and carries the daily habit, 39% of five-year revenue in the middle case; if its rules tighten we move to ₹100+ fund debits, which earn far less. And a big app could simply do this first. Likely question: Which worries you most? Answer: Savers stopping. Riders change platforms often, and any UPI app can now move their mandate.
