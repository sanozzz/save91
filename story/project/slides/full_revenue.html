Say: This is revenue per saver who is still active, by year of the saver's life, in the base case. In year 2 it is ₹438: gold margin, loan fees and insurance commission make up almost all of it. The liquid fund earns ₹2 and FDs ₹5 per saver a year, which is why the brief's claim 5 holds: savings alone does not pay. Loan fees here assume our 5% loss cover, which lets lenders pay us about 1.5% plus 5.5% of each loan instead of 1.5%. In the best case the same saver brings ₹1,865 in year 2. Jar is the reference point: its filed FY25 revenue was ₹2,447.8 cr, mostly gold resold; its own 'operating revenue' figure was ₹208 cr, which cannot be rebuilt from the filing; and it still lost ₹50.5 cr. Likely question: Why does revenue fall after year 2? Answer: Loan take-up saturates among the savers who stay.
