Save first, lend later

Does the story add up? Not yet. Here is what would make it add up.

Saving first makes customers cheap to win and needs almost no capital. But it pays only if three things go right together: savers stay, daily debits get cheap, and savers go on to buy loans and insurance. A one-year pilot can settle all three.

Say: This is the story in nine slides; the full numbers deck follows as the appendix. The idea is a micro-savings app for delivery and ride workers and for shopkeepers and the self-employed, in equal numbers. They save ₹40 to ₹50 a day automatically, partners hold the money, and we earn later by selling loans and insurance. The short answer to whether it adds up: in the middle case, no; each saver loses money. It becomes a strong business only when three things go right together, and a one-year pilot can test all three before any big money goes in. Likely question: So is the idea dead? Answer: No. It is unproven. The best case is real if the three readings come in.
