What we found, in five numbers

The race is not to sign the most savers. It is to keep committed savers, and to prove their saving predicts repayment before anyone else does.

1 · Entry is clear: anyone can build the ground floor

Copying Bachatt's ✓ squares gets us parity at best. The market will be won on the contested floors.
Savings trail is under ₹1 a user a year at Bachatt's visible balances (~₹167 a user). One loan is worth more than years of trail.

2 · Savings is the hook, not the business

Small-ticket savers leave apps and SIPs fast; the one doorstep collector we spoke to keeps most for 5+ years. Bachatt's 3M is sign-ups: it publishes no active count.
Savings can't repay what a saver costs to win. Whoever wins has to climb the credit ladder, with savers who stay long enough to borrow.

3 · The money is in credit; the edge is the signal

A licence alone doesn't make an NBFC profitable: fintech NBFCs keep about 1% after losses. Credit quality decides it.
If saving predicts repayment, the player with deeper savers wins on credit. If it doesn't, this is a thin-fee lead business. Test it first.

4 · Who else is here, and what they might do

We can't out-reach PhonePe or out-spend Jar. Our room is depth: committed savers in one segment, and the assisted lane no app plays in.

5 · Offline is a 20% test, not the bet

Offline is Bachatt's empty square. We test it on ~20% of savers and grow it only where it pays for itself, mostly through credit.
