This is the whole business in one line per customer. Winning a customer costs ₹475. Over five years their savings earn us ₹49, and everything else we could sell them adds ₹113: loans of every kind, mutual-fund SIPs, insurance, pension, an FD-backed card, bills and recharges, priced product by product in the appendix. Serving them costs ₹257, most of it the fee on every debit. So a customer is worth −₹95 before we even count the cost of winning them, and −₹570 after. We never get the ₹475 back, even over ten years. That is generous: selling starts in month one and its own costs are left out. Only if every product hit its best case at the same time would a customer pay back, in month 32, and even then they only just break even over five years.
