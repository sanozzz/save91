THE DEFINITION

One saver's value is three streams (the fund, loans, other products) for as long as they keep saving, less serving, against the cost to win them

Saver won, not sign-upA saver is someone who starts saving. Online, 1 in 5 sign-ups does; the cost to win is everything spent to get one saver.
Undiscounted, 5 years (8 alongside)LTV = what a saver brings in less what it costs to serve them while they save. No discounting: the cumulative rupees.
The cautious saverThe answer is for a saver who joins early, before take-up and approvals have grown with the business's track record (they grow to year 5), at the post-seed ₹450 to win. Later savers are worth more.
Words used here. KYC'd: finished the identity check. Trail: the yearly share of a fund's fee paid to its distributor. SIP: a regular instalment into a mutual fund. NBFC: a non-bank lender. DSA: an agent paid by a lender for referred loans. EMI: a loan instalment. LAP: a loan against property. Doorstep: a field agent signs the saver in person. The seed: the first 18 months. Futures: simulated draws of all inputs together.
Not in this view: the team, office and brand, the 10% contingency, the two-month wait for fees, tax. The app never holds the money; loans are made by banks and NBFCs.

WHEN IT PAYS BACK

The online saver earns back the ₹450 in month 19; the doorstep saver in month 23; by year 8 each has returned about six and a half times its cost

Why month 19Loans start in month 1 for every KYC'd user, at half the full take-up and 60% of the approvals for six months; a first loan pays 2% of ₹1 lakh. The fund trail builds slowly with the balance.
A jump in month 7The first six months run at half the take-up and 60% of the approvals; from month 7 every user applies at the full rate, so first loans peak, then thin as users borrow once and savers stop.
Then a long tailAfter year 1, 1.5% of savers stop each month; the fund balance, repeat borrowing and loan offers to KYC'd users who never saved keep paying: ₹1,105 more in years 6–8.
Left: cumulative revenue less serving, minus the cost to win (Saver tab, the last column of each saver). Right: the online saver's monthly revenue lines, stacked.

ONE INPUT AT A TIME

Six inputs leadLoan size, how many apply, the cost to win and the daily amount each move LTV/CAC by about 3 turns across their ranges; the payout and how many keep saving by about 2.

A11 · HOW THE MODEL WORKS

The month-by-month rules behind one saver's value

StepHow it is worked out, every month
Who keeps savingA steady monthly drop-off within months 1–3, another within months 4–12 (set by the share at month 3 and at month 12), then 1.5% a month.
The fundBalance = last month's balance less 1% withdrawn, plus deposits, plus the blended return on both. Our trail = average balance × 0.67% ÷ 12.
LoansEach reachable user is in one of three states: never borrowed, repaid and able to borrow again, or repaying. Each month a share applies (35% a year spread over 12 months, × the take-up factor); approved first loans move to repaying; 18 months later they can borrow again.
Top-upsBorrowers with 12+ EMIs paid and EMIs still due: 15% a year take a top-up of 40% of the original loan, at most one per loan.
PayoutsMoney lent × the payout (2% rising in a straight line to 3% by month 60) × (1 − 2.5% taken back). Counted for 2.5 KYC'd users per online saver.
ProductsFor each product, the share not yet holding it buys at a monthly rate (the yearly take-up spread over 12 months × the take-up factor). Policies renew yearly at their renewal rate; habits keep running; insurance pays the partner's share.
FactorsTake-up factor: 50% in months 1–6, then 100%, × 1.05 per business year to year 5 (cap 2×). First-loan approval: 30% × 60% in months 1–6 × 1.10 per year (cap 80%).
Serving and paybackServing = (₹5 + ₹1) × share still saving. Cumulative = −cost to win + Σ(revenue − serving); pays back in the first month it reaches zero.
These are the rules on the workbook's Engine, Products and Saver tabs, one column each.
